# Sector research: product and hybrid businesses (group B)

November 2026. These entries say how each sector really markets on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and paid
social, so Studio pictures show the business itself and not a polished stock scene. Everything is in our
own words. Entries never name a brand (brand names appear only in the Sources table). Proof lists hold
only what a business can supply itself: its own photos, footage, specs, certificates, warranty terms and
real customer posts shared with permission. Nothing here invents a review, a number or a person.

Method note: research used web search summaries of public pages (platform case studies, agency and trade
write-ups, regulator guidance). Direct page fetches were blocked by the network on several hosts in this
session, so a few points rest on the search summaries of those pages. Vendor and platform performance
figures were treated as directional and are not repeated in the entries.

## Cross-sector lessons

1. **Show it working before you name it.** Across electronics, appliances, phone accessories, kitchenware
   and cleaning, the strongest creative is the product doing its job in the first second (a drop test, food
   sliding off a pan, a stain lifting). A static product on a gradient reads as a catalogue, not a story.
2. **The real place is the proof.** For hybrid businesses (dealers, pharmacies, hardware stores,
   bookshops, solar installers) the shop floor, lot, counter, roof or bay is what makes a post believable.
   Studio should prefer the business's own photos and frame them, not redraw a generic interior.
3. **Process beats polish for anything made.** Furniture, textiles, handicrafts and sports goods earn
   trust from workshop and making footage. Hands, tools and raw material are the picture.
4. **Answer the specific doubt.** Almost every sector has one dominant fear: fit (parts, cases, sofas),
   genuineness (chargers, medicines, solar panels), safety (baby, toys, pets, cleaning), or delivery
   (online stores, gifts). The best headline speaks to that doubt with something the business can show.
5. **Real people, real pets, with consent.** Customer posts shared with permission are the honest version
   of social proof. Studio must never invent customers, reviews, chats or figures; it can only frame
   content the business supplies.
6. **Regulated sectors need guard rails in code.** Toys (target parents, no pester power), baby care
   (no medical claims, formula limits), pharmacy (no prescription-only promotion, careful health claims),
   dealers (special ad category, full finance terms) and solar (no promised returns under changing
   net-metering rules).
7. **Culture moments drive retail.** Eid, Ramadan, Diwali, wedding season, back to school, 11.11 and
   Black Friday matter for almost every product sector; heat, monsoon and load-shedding matter for
   appliances, solar and parts in South Asia and the Gulf.
8. **Creators and organic first, then paid.** Platform case studies repeatedly describe turning strong
   organic or creator posts into ads. For a small business the equivalent is the owner's or staff's own
   footage, made the hero of the ad.
