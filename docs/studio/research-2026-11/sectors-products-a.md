# Sector research: product businesses, group A (2026-11)

How the best brands and agencies really market each sector on Instagram, TikTok,
Facebook and paid ads, written so Studio can make pictures that ring true for the
business instead of polished but generic ones. Each block is data (shape fixed,
lengths enforced by tests). Everything is paraphrased; no brand is named inside an
entry. Sources are listed at the end.

Common thread from the sources: the product itself, in the place it is really used,
shown up close enough to read the material, beats a mood picture. Creator-style,
real-use footage beats studio polish on TikTok and Reels. Proof is what the business
holds: its own photos, its own making process, its own counts and real permitted reviews.

## Cross-sector lessons

- **The real product in the real place of use wins.** Platform research (TikTok
  food and drink) and every sector guide agree: put the product at the centre, then
  the moment of use. Mood scenes with no product readable are what make posts generic.
- **Close enough to read the material.** Texture swatches (skincare), macro settings
  (jewellery, watches), grain and stitching (leather), print in daylight (lawn) and
  cross-sections (cakes) answer the buyer's main doubt better than any headline.
- **Fit and scale on a body.** Clothes, shoes, jewellery, watches and eyewear all sell
  on "how it looks on someone like me": on-hand, on-wrist, on-feet, several sizes,
  several skin tones, several face shapes.
- **Show the making.** Behind-the-scenes process (roasting, baking, arranging, leather
  bench, workshop assembly) is named the strongest content type for small makers.
- **Answer the doubt in the creative.** Size guides, return policy, roast date, lab
  test, ingredient list, delivery cut-off: put it on a slide, not only in the caption.
- **Honest beats polished on Reels and TikTok.** Creator-style, real-use footage
  (wash day, try-on, first sip, unboxing) beats studio polish; blowouts passed as
  air-dried, heavy filters and invented results break trust and often policy.
- **Culture-true moments.** Modest fashion and lawn live by Ramadan, Eid and wedding
  season; critics note campaigns fail when made by people who do not live the moment,
  and when styling breaks modesty. Use family, iftar and everyday wear, not only
  lanterns and crescents.
- **Lawn brands look alike.** Commentary on the lawn market says many posts are
  unrecognisable without the logo; the flat-lay of every piece plus the brand's own
  shoot style is what differentiates.
- **Legal lines.** Supplements and health-adjacent beauty: no disease claims, no
  weight-loss before/after, claims must be substantiated, under-18 targeting limits.
- **Label legibility.** For packaged food and drink, condensation, splashes and props
  must never cover the brand, flavour or size.
