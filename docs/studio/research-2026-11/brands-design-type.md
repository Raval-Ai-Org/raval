# Big-brand craft, global design languages and type voices (research, 2026-11)

Why this exists: Studio's posts lean on one heavy condensed sans and read as
templates. This file collects (1) what large brands actually do on social and
in campaigns, distilled into techniques a small business can use honestly,
(2) about a dozen regional and historical design languages described in words
an image model follows, and (3) a type-voice library for the 27 ids Mellox
will use, each checked against the Google Fonts catalogue.

Rules followed: study only, no brand is to be named in a prompt or a recipe;
everything is in our own words; quotes are a few words at most. Confidence is
marked where the public record is thin (`[thin]`) or where a point is our own
observation of a feed rather than a published write-up (`[observed]`).

## Part 1 — Big-brand social and campaign craft

Format per brand: **Platform** (the one idea that runs across pieces) ·
**Visual system** · **Feed** (format mix; product vs culture vs humour) ·
**Transferable** (one line, brand-neutral, honest for a small business).

### Apple
- **Platform:** "Shot on iPhone" — the product is proven by what customers made
  with it. Launched 2015 with photos found on public sites, credited to the
  photographer; Outdoor Grand Prix at Cannes 2015 and a Creative
  Effectiveness Grand Prix in 2025 for running a decade (MacRumors, TNW).
- **Visual system:** the photo is the whole canvas; a tiny caption line
  ("Shot on…" plus the person's name) sits in a corner; white or black field,
  one quiet grotesk, enormous margins on product posts.
- **Feed:** almost no promotional copy; real-world results, product on
  seamless colour fields at launches, short film.
- **Transferable:** *proof-by-output* — show a real result the customer made
  (or the work you did for them), full bleed, credited with permission, a
  one-line caption and nothing else.

### Samsung
- **Platform:** the challenger line — "Join the Flip Side" (2022) invited people
  "loyal" to a rival to switch, showing the foldable in everyday life
  (Contagious, MacRumors).
- **Visual system:** clean product renders, deep blue/black fields, the
  product's distinctive gesture (folding) as the recurring device.
- **Feed:** launch hero renders, feature demos, comparative humour.
- **Transferable:** *show the one gesture only your product has* (it folds, it
  pours, it opens) as the image; never mock a named rival — comparative jabs
  age badly when the rival catches up (ContentGrip notes ads quietly
  withdrawn once the brand dropped the same feature).

### Nike
- **Platform:** "Just Do It" (since 1988) — the athlete's story carries the
  brand's promise of possibility (Sprout Social).
- **Visual system:** a heavy geometric condensed sans for the line, usually
  uppercase; black/white with photography doing the colour; the swoosh small.
- **Feed:** "a digital brand magazine" of athlete narratives rather than ads;
  product mostly worn, in motion.
- **Transferable:** *the customer is the hero* — one real person mid-action,
  one short imperative or statement, brand mark small. Use real customers or
  staff with consent, never implied endorsements.

### Adidas
- **Platform:** "Impossible Is Nothing" relaunched 2021 with documentary-style
  athlete films; replaced in 2024 by "You Got This", aimed at everyday
  athletes and the pressure they feel (adidas-group.com, The Drum).
- **Visual system:** three stripes as a framing/graphic device, black/white
  with one accent, documentary photography.
- **Feed:** heritage product (terrace and lifestyle), athletes, street culture.
- **Transferable:** *own a graphic device from your mark* (stripes, a shape, a
  corner) and repeat it as framing on every post so the feed reads as one.

### McDonald's
- **Platform (case):** "Follow the Arches" (Cossette, Canada 2018) — cropped
  pieces of the logo as wayfinding billboards with one instruction line; the
  agency's point was that the shape is recognisable even in pieces
  (Adweek, The Drum, Cannes Lions).
- **Visual system:** brand yellow + red, crop of the mark, almost no words;
  food photography that is extremely close and appetising.
- **Feed:** product close-ups, menu moments, fan culture and memes.
- **Transferable:** *crop your own mark as the image* — a fragment of the
  logo or signature product shape on a flat brand colour, one 2–5 word line.
  Only works once people already know the mark; new businesses should use
  the whole product instead.

### KFC
- **Platform (case):** the 2018 UK chicken shortage apology — an empty bucket
  with letters rearranged to "FCK" and a plain apology underneath (Mother;
  One Show, Cannes PR; It's Nice That).
- **Visual system:** red/white, the bucket as icon, irreverent copy.
- **Feed:** product, humour, self-aware brand jokes.
- **Transferable:** *own your mistake in one honest image* — when something
  goes wrong (sold out, delayed, closed), say so plainly with a light touch
  on your own product, never spin. Never fake a crisis for attention.

### Burger King
- **Platform:** the 2021 rebrand by Jones Knowles Ritchie, the first in about
  20 years — "real food" cues, retro-modern, everything from packaging to
  digital (Design Week, Dezeen, Commarts).
- **Visual system:** a custom chunky rounded serif inspired by the brand's own
  archive of soft bold serifs, letterforms "rounded, bold, yummy"; colours
  named after flame-grilling (red, orange, barbecue brown); flat food
  illustration (Design Week, Creative Bloq, D&AD "Logos, Your Way").
- **Feed:** cheeky rival jabs, flat graphic food, provocative copy.
- **Transferable:** *type that looks like the product* — choose a letter shape
  that echoes what you sell (soft and round for food, rigid for tools) and
  colours named after your process; consistency does the rest.

### Coca-Cola
- **Platform:** "Share a Coke" (Ogilvy Sydney, 2011/12) — first names on the
  label in place of the logo; a custom typeface was drawn because of the
  script logo's trademark concerns; rolled to 80+ countries (Campaign Brief,
  Coca-Cola Company, The Drum).
- **Visual system:** brand red, the dynamic ribbon, the bottle silhouette,
  warm people photography.
- **Feed:** moments of togetherness, seasonal (holiday trucks, polar
  bears), product sweat-drop close-ups.
- **Transferable:** *personal-name hook* — put the customer's name or their
  town/street on the product in the picture. Only with names they gave you
  or generic common names; never a real private person's photo.

### Starbucks
- **Platform:** seasonal rituals — the holiday red cup, red-dominant since
  1999 and a marketing event since 2005; 2025 used hand-drawn animation
  rather than a new drink (Starbucks Stories, Wikipedia).
- **Visual system:** siren green with a seasonal accent (cranberry red, soft
  pastels in 2025), illustration on cups, warm lifestyle photography.
- **Feed:** drink close-ups, seasonal menu drops, customer cup photos.
- **Transferable:** *a recurring seasonal object* — pick one thing (a cup, a
  bag, a box) that changes design each season and announce the change as an
  event. Typeface details not confirmed in sources `[thin]`.

### IKEA
- **Platform:** affordable, democratic design; recent work on resale and
  second life (Second Hand campaign, Oslo 2014, with real sellers' items and
  phone numbers; Buy Back Friday) (Ads of the World, Cannes Lions, Edelman).
- **Visual system:** blue/yellow, product as the joke or the argument; print
  ads with one object, a line, a price.
- **Feed:** room sets, small-space hacks, witty one-liners about home life.
- **Transferable:** *product + price + one human truth* — one item photographed
  plainly, its real price large, one line about a real household moment.

### Spotify
- **Platform:** Wrapped (yearly since 2016) — each person's own listening data
  turned into shareable cards; 2024 used the custom Spotify Mix typeface,
  which ranges from ultra-bold to condensed skinny lettering
  (Spotify newsroom, Fast Company, D&AD).
- **Visual system:** clashing duotone pairs, huge numerals, layered free-flowing
  shapes that adapt across 170 markets (D&AD).
- **Feed:** data stories, artist culture, playful copy about listening habits.
- **Transferable:** *personal data wrap* — a year-end card from the business's
  own real numbers ("412 cakes baked, most-ordered: lemon"). Needs real data;
  never invent figures. `needs-data`

### Netflix
- **Platform:** the show is the content; one consistent fan voice across
  dozens of regional accounts (School of Social; Erasmus theses on US,
  German, Indian/Dutch Instagram). Netflix India's meme campaign is a WARC
  case.
- **Visual system:** stills and frames from titles with meme-style captions;
  red/black only as signature.
- **Feed:** memes, cast moments, reaction posts, release dates.
- **Transferable:** *talk like a fan of your own product* — caption a real
  moment from your work (a photo from the kitchen, the workshop) the way a
  customer would joke about it.

### Duolingo
- **Platform:** the mascot as an "unhinged" character; TikTok grew from about
  50k to 16 million followers in four years; even staged the owl's "death"
  as a stunt (The Drum, Fast Company, Digiday).
- **Visual system:** bright green, the owl in costume, lo-fi phone video;
  very little designed type.
- **Feed:** native-platform skits, trend sounds, replies to comments; the
  joke started with users teasing the reminder notifications.
- **Transferable:** *reply-culture* — the brand reacts in character to a real
  comment or real moment, quickly, on the platform's own terms. Needs a
  consistent persona; risky for regulated or grief-adjacent businesses.

### Airbnb
- **Platform:** belonging; the 2014 DesignStudio identity built the Bélo, a
  symbol anyone could draw, plus a tool for people to make their own
  (Dezeen, Design Week). 2018 custom Cereal typeface with Dalton Maag
  (It's Nice That, AIGA Eye on Design).
- **Visual system:** coral accent, rounded friendly sans, real home and host
  photography.
- **Feed:** remarkable listings, hosts' stories, travel moments.
- **Transferable:** *the place is the post* — the most specific, real detail of
  the space (a window seat, the view) as the whole picture, with the host or
  owner's first-person line.

### Google
- **Platform:** Year in Search — the year told through rising queries, built
  on Google Trends data with a published methodology; 2024–25 shifted to
  fandoms and a bottom-up ecosystem (Shorty Awards, about.google, Campaign).
- **Visual system:** the search bar as a device; primary colours; emotional
  documentary footage.
- **Feed:** doodles, product tips, data stories.
- **Transferable:** *the question customers asked most* — design a post around
  a real frequently asked question, typed as a search or chat bubble, then
  answer it. Uses the business's own enquiries. `needs-data` (light)

### Red Bull
- **Platform:** "gives you wings" lived as media: Red Bull Media House (2007)
  runs the brand's social and owns events; Stratos (2012) drew about 1 million
  accounts into the conversation (Contently, Dachis Group via Slideshare).
- **Visual system:** the can is small or absent; the athlete or event is the
  picture; silver/blue/red only on the product.
- **Feed:** extreme sport stills and clips, almost no product shots.
- **Transferable:** *the brand as host* — post the event, workshop or community
  moment you actually ran, not the product. Needs real events.
  `needs-proof`

### LEGO
- **Platform:** "Rebuild the World" (2019, first global brand platform in about
  30 years), creative problem solving through play (LEGO press, AEF case,
  X case study).
- **Visual system:** primary colours, the brick as building block of every
  image, playful builds that turn into scenes.
- **Feed:** fan builds, sets in imaginative scenes, behind-the-design.
- **Transferable:** *build the message from your own material* — spell the
  word or shape the scene with the thing you sell or use (flour, thread,
  tiles). Must be physically real or plausibly makeable.

### Glossier
- **Platform:** skin first, peer recommendation; the feed as a mood board,
  packaging designed to be photographed (Harvard D3 cases, Social Media Today).
- **Visual system:** a single signature pale pink, white space, soft daylight,
  minimal sans; product on skin, not on a podium.
- **Feed:** reposts of customers (credited), product textures, staff faces.
- **Transferable:** *one signature tint everywhere* — choose one soft brand
  colour and let it appear in every frame (backdrop, prop, garment) with
  real faces and credited customer photos.

### Gymshark
- **Platform:** community-made progress — early creator partnerships on
  YouTube, the #Gymshark66 66-day habit challenge producing masses of
  user content (LibreTexts case, Kolsquare, WARC).
- **Visual system:** black/white/grey, gym photography, athletic bodies in
  product, bold sans.
- **Feed:** athletes, challenges, transformations, community events.
- **Transferable:** *a dated challenge* — a fixed-length challenge (7, 30, 66
  days) with a simple daily post template people can repeat. Before/after
  claims need consent and honesty; avoid body-shaming.

### Liquid Death
- **Platform:** treat a commodity (canned water) like a heavy-metal band:
  "Murder Your Thirst", skull/gothic marks, tallboy cans (Print, Beloved Brands).
- **Visual system:** black-letter/gothic logo, metal-poster illustration,
  deadpan absurdist copy.
- **Feed:** absurd sketches, mock-serious product claims, merch.
- **Transferable:** *borrow a genre's clothes* — present an everyday product in
  the visual grammar of an unexpected world (metal poster, nature
  documentary, luxury ad). Only if the whole brand commits; one-off it reads
  as a gimmick.

### Notion
- **Platform:** "Think it. Make it." (2024, with Buck) built on the brand's
  hand-drawn people; originals by Roman Muradov were black and white, the
  campaign added primary colour (Notion blog, Buck, Design Compass).
- **Visual system:** gestural ink line drawings, pure black on white, colour
  used sparingly; product UI screenshots.
- **Feed:** templates, UI tips, community setups.
- **Transferable:** *one hand-drawn line style* — a consistent ink-line
  illustration language in one colour, with lots of white. Do not imitate a
  named illustrator's style.

### Canva
- **Platform:** "Love Your Work" (2024) — real users instead of actors, made
  by a 29-person in-house team (The Drum, afaqs, Campaign Asia).
- **Visual system:** bright gradients, playful layouts, real people at work.
- **Feed:** templates, tutorials, before/after designs, workplace humour.
- **Transferable:** *real customers, real work* — your actual customers (with
  permission) holding or using what you made for them, named.

### Emirates
- **Platform:** "Hello Tomorrow" (2012, StrawberryFrog; print by Adam Taylor)
  and the 2021 stunt of a real cabin crew member on top of the Burj Khalifa,
  shot without green screen (Campaign Brief, Khaleej Times, Cannes Lions).
- **Visual system:** red/gold, cream and white, luxury photography, generous
  space, bilingual signage.
- **Feed:** cabin details, destinations, cinematic stunts, hospitality rituals.
- **Transferable:** *one true, impressive fact shown, not told* — stage the
  real thing (the real view, the real height, the real hand-finishing) and
  say "no tricks" only if there were none. `needs-proof`

### Regional leaders

**Zomato (India).** Platform: a casual, witty voice in notifications and
social; puns, memes and moment marketing (Social Samosa; markhub24 notes
engagement figures are rarely documented). Visual: red, food photos, text-
only cards. Transferable: *push-notification wit* — a one-line, conversational
card that reads like a message from the business ("Your usual is lonely").
Critiques note humour builds love more reliably than sales.

**Swiggy / Instamart (India).** Platform: moment marketing (cricket, viral
challenges) and fast in-character replies; "Khali Ka Khaali Cart"
complete-the-line DM campaign by Schbang (exchange4media, Business Standard).
Transferable: *fill-in-the-blank prompt* — post an unfinished line about your
product and ask followers to finish it.

**Amul (India).** Platform: the Amul girl topical — since 1966–67 a cartoon
mascot comments on the week's news with a pun; now several a week
(ThePrint, Muse by Clios, YourStory). Visual: one consistent illustration
style, hand lettering, red/white, the pack shot small. Transferable:
*the weekly topical cartoon* — the same character or object reacts to a real
public moment with a pun, every week. Avoid tragedy, politics and other
brands' trademarks; Amul itself drew official complaints.

**Careem (Gulf).** Platform: "everything app" — 2023 refresh kept the green and
the "C" wink, rolled out in-house from app to rider boxes; self-reported
70% rise in understanding of multi-service (Campaign ME, Cannes entry).
Transferable: *your mark on your real touchpoints* — show the logo on the
uniform, the bag, the van, the receipt: the brand in the street.

**Khaadi (Pakistan).** Platform: seasonal lawn and Eid collections; a 2021
lawn campaign noted for including plus-size models; social-first scarf
styling video (Dawn Aurora, Dawn Images). `[thin]` Visual `[observed]`:
textile print full-bleed, models outdoors in soft light, the collection name
in a thin serif or spaced caps. Transferable: *the print is the poster* — let
the real fabric, pattern or material fill the frame, with a small collection
name.

**Daraz (South Asia).** Platform: 11.11 mega-sale, now multi-day; growth
outside the big cities emphasised; figures are company releases
(ProPakistani, Dhaka Tribune, TBS). Visual `[observed]`: orange, the date as
a giant numeral, countdowns, dense price badges. Transferable: *the date is
the headline* — a real sale date as giant numerals with one offer line; only
real discounts and deadlines.

**foodpanda (Asia).** Platform: pink since 2017 (from orange, to stand out in
orange-heavy streets); "live like a panda" philosophy with the Pau-Pau mascot
in 2023 (Marketing-Interactive, Coconuts, Transform). Transferable:
*own a colour your category doesn't use* — pick the hue no competitor in
your street uses and flood it.

**Trendyol (Türkiye).** `[thin]` No official rebrand write-up found; a
temporary orange-to-green swap for Saudi National Day is recorded (Okaz).
Visual `[observed]`: orange, campaign codes, big percentage badges, product
grids. Transferable: *a local-occasion colour swap* — for a national or local
day, change the brand colour of the post (respectfully) rather than adding
flags and clip art.

## Part 2 — Global design languages

Each: typography · colour · grid/layout · copy tone · suits · **description**
(two lines an image model can follow, no brand or designer named).

### 1. Swiss / International Typographic Style
- **Type:** neutral grotesk sans, flush left, ragged right, few sizes, strong
  weight contrast between headline and text.
- **Colour:** black, white, one primary (red) or none; photography often black
  and white (Swiss National Library).
- **Grid:** mathematical modular grid, asymmetric placement, objective
  photography instead of illustration (Lumen/OpenText design history).
- **Copy:** factual, short, informational.
- **Suits:** architects, engineering, SaaS, museums, consultancies.
- **Description:** "Strict modular grid with asymmetric, flush-left sans-serif
  type and a single bold red accent on white. Objective black-and-white
  photograph, generous margins, no ornament."

### 2. Japanese poster
- **Type:** small, widely spaced type; occasionally one enormous character;
  vertical lines of text are common in Japanese layouts (not confirmed by our
  sources) `[observed]`.
- **Colour:** minimal palettes, effective use of black, a single strong hue
  (Cooper Hewitt).
- **Grid:** "ma": empty space is structural, not left over; a few objects in a
  large open field. A maximalist strand (dense collage) also exists.
- **Copy:** poetic, seasonal, understated.
- **Suits:** tea, ceramics, craft, skincare, galleries, minimalist food.
- **Description:** "One small object placed off-centre in a vast calm field,
  a single strong colour against off-white. Tiny, widely tracked lettering in
  a narrow column; silence is the subject."

### 3. Korean social
- **Type:** bold rounded or geometric sans for headlines, playful stickers,
  dense card-news text layers; experimental poster type in the design scene
  (Creative Bloq, Eye on Design).
- **Colour:** bright contrasting colours on neutral backgrounds; pastel plus
  one saturated pop; retro web nods (It's Nice That on K-pop).
- **Grid:** card-news carousels: big headline slide, numbered information
  slides, sticker-like labels and arrows.
- **Copy:** cute, direct, list-like, emoji-light in design.
- **Suits:** cafés, beauty, stationery, fashion, edtech.
- **Description:** "Bright pastel background with one saturated accent, a big
  rounded bold headline and small sticker-style labels with arrows. Clean,
  cute, card-like layout with crisp product cut-outs."

### 4. Scandinavian minimal
- **Type:** clean sans, text-led, modest sizes, sentence case.
- **Colour:** light neutral palette (oat, stone, pale wood) plus one muted
  accent; nature-inspired (DesignBro, Smashing).
- **Grid:** reductive, function first, lots of air; product in daylight.
- **Copy:** calm, honest, practical, democratic.
- **Suits:** furniture, interiors, coffee, wellness, sustainable products.
- **Description:** "Soft daylight on a pale wood and oat-coloured set, one
  object, lots of quiet space. Small clean sans-serif text in sentence case,
  one muted sage or rust accent."

### 5. US direct response
- **Type:** big benefit headline, subhead, bullets, price/offer burst, button;
  heavy sans or slab.
- **Colour:** high contrast; red/yellow for offers; white backgrounds.
- **Grid:** hook → promise → evidence → call to action; few elements, the
  offer and the action unmissable (Writer's Digest, NAU course notes).
- **Copy:** benefit-first, specific, a deadline if real.
- **Suits:** e-commerce, local services, courses, gyms, clinics (careful).
- **Description:** "A bold benefit headline across the top, the product large
  on white, three short tick-mark points and a bright pill-shaped button.
  High-contrast, uncluttered, every element points to the action."

### 6. UK witty, copy-led
- **Type:** one line of serif or clean sans, set large, often white out of a
  brand colour; minimal or no image. The Economist's white-on-red posters
  since 1988 (AMV), shaped by the masthead (InPublishing, Dave Dye).
- **Colour:** a single brand colour field.
- **Grid:** the sentence is the layout; logo small in a corner.
- **Copy:** dry, clever, assumes intelligence; one thought.
- **Suits:** publishers, banks, insurers, B2B, bars, bookshops.
- **Description:** "A single witty sentence in white on a solid brand-colour
  field, set large with generous margins. No picture, logo small in a corner."

### 7. Latin / Brazilian vibrant
- **Type:** bold display, playful mixed weights, super-graphics; tropical
  modernist references.
- **Colour:** saturated, joyful combinations (green, yellow, magenta,
  cobalt), abstract shapes (WeTransfer on David Galasse, It's Nice That).
- **Grid:** layered colour blocks, big shapes cropping off the edges.
- **Copy:** warm, celebratory, colloquial.
- **Suits:** food, drinks, festivals, fashion, tourism, music.
- **Description:** "Saturated tropical colour blocks in green, yellow and
  magenta with bold playful lettering and large abstract shapes bleeding off
  the edges. Joyful, sunny, energetic."

### 8. Gulf / Arabic bilingual luxury
- **Type:** Arabic and Latin set as equals; high-contrast Latin serif or
  spaced caps with calligraphic or geometric Kufi Arabic; Arabic must not be
  aligned to Latin cap height because its tails fall below the baseline
  (bilingual type guides; Hertfordshire thesis on bi-scriptual typography;
  29LT on a luxury Arabic logo).
- **Colour:** gold, sand, black, cream, deep green or burgundy.
- **Grid:** symmetric, centred, generous space, right-to-left aware.
- **Copy:** formal, hospitable, refined.
- **Suits:** perfume, jewellery, hospitality, real estate, abayas, dates.
- **Description:** "Centred, symmetrical luxury layout on cream and black with
  thin gold rules. A refined headline set twice, in elegant Arabic and in
  spaced Latin capitals of equal visual weight."

### 9. South-Asian festive retail (Pakistan / India)
- **Type:** decorative display or bold rounded sans, often bilingual (Urdu /
  Hindi with English); big offer numbers.
- **Colour:** gold, red, saffron, magenta, deep green; marigold, diyas,
  rangoli and mango-leaf motifs (Kimp, GoDaddy India). Better campaigns keep
  visuals clean so the story leads (archedu on Cadbury 2024).
- **Grid:** symmetric frames and borders, centred offer, product in the middle.
- **Copy:** warm wishes plus the offer; family and gifting.
- **Suits:** fashion, sweets, jewellery, electronics, grocery around Eid,
  Diwali, wedding season.
- **Description:** "A festive frame of marigold strings and soft lamp-glow on
  deep red and gold, the product centred. One warm greeting and a clear offer
  in bold rounded type; ornament at the edges only."

### 10. Turkish retail
`[thin]` — public write-ups on Turkish discount-campaign graphics were not
found; this is an observed convention, verify before use.
- **Type:** bold rounded geometric sans, big percentage numerals.
- **Colour:** one saturated brand colour (orange, purple) with a contrasting
  accent (yellow); flat backgrounds (Getir purple + yellow per brand guides).
- **Grid:** product grid or single hero with a large discount badge,
  campaign code chip.
- **Copy:** short, urgent, playful.
- **Suits:** e-commerce, quick-commerce, grocery, fashion.
- **Description:** "One saturated brand-colour background with a cut-out
  product and a big round percentage badge in a contrasting colour. Bold
  rounded sans, short punchy offer line, flat and bright."

### 11. Editorial magazine
- **Type:** display serif headline, deck, body, caption ladder; pull quotes
  with hanging marks; oversized serif headlines anchor rhythm; two families
  at most (Creative Bloq's editorial rules, Smashing on Ernest Journal).
- **Colour:** paper white, ink black, one accent drawn from the photo.
- **Grid:** columns, white space, a scaled-down photo framed by space.
- **Copy:** narrative, interview-style, a strong quote.
- **Suits:** consultants, restaurants with a story, fashion, architecture,
  founders' content.
- **Description:** "A magazine page: large elegant serif headline, a short
  italic deck and a framed photograph with generous white margins. Thin rules,
  a small caption, paper-white background."

### 12. Brutalist / raw
- **Type:** heavy sans, often capitals, used structurally; system-font feel;
  text bleeding off the edge, rotated blocks (NN/g on brutalism, Linearity).
- **Colour:** stark black/white with one loud colour; neo-brutalism adds neon
  and thick outlines (NN/g neobrutalism).
- **Grid:** visible grid, then broken on purpose; crop marks, kraft textures.
- **Copy:** blunt, unapologetic.
- **Suits:** streetwear, music, galleries, dev tools, indie zines.
- **Description:** "Raw poster: heavy black capitals on a stark white field,
  thick borders, one loud neon block and text that runs off the edge. Visible
  grid lines, deliberately unpolished."

## AI-made design tells to avoid

Sourced from practitioner write-ups (Towards AI, UX Planet, Venngage,
Mika Reyes, Clutch, LinkedIn designers); treat as opinion, but they match
what reviewers flag in Mellox output.

1. **Garbled or near-miss text** — still the most reliable tell; zoom in on
   every word (Clutch / Lovart guides). Mellox: exact words only, checked.
2. **Default styling** — purple gradients, glassmorphism, 3D blobs, emoji
   icons, centered hero-plus-three-cards (Towards AI).
3. **Uniform detail** — everything equally sharp and textured, no focal
   point (detection guides).
4. **Unchosen colour** — a colour present because one was asked for, not
   because it means something (designer on LinkedIn).
5. **Font salad** — script + brush + block mixed because each looked fine
   alone (Mika Reyes).
6. **Ultra-conventional hierarchy** — the same skeleton repeated on every
   piece (UX Planet). This is the Mellox "templated" problem.
7. **The same heavy condensed caps on everything** — our own observation:
   one type voice for every brand erases the brand.
8. **Over-polished everything** — glossy rim light, perfect symmetry, plastic
   skin; real brands mix in honest phone photos (Duolingo, Glossier cases).
9. **Ornament overload** — every festive symbol at once (archedu on Diwali
   campaigns).
10. Detection is getting harder; the fix is the same either way: remove
    defaults, choose on purpose (Towards AI).

## Principles top designers use for social posts

1. **One focal point.** A single dominant element, everything else
   secondary (Smashing Magazine, "Dominance, focal points and hierarchy").
2. **Hierarchy through scale, value, colour, spacing, placement** — a small,
   medium, large mix (Nielsen Norman Group, "Principles of visual design").
3. **Scale contrast, not size.** Scale is relative; contrast in scale creates
   emphasis (Shopify on visual hierarchy).
4. **Contrast makes focus.** The element that differs is the one seen first
   (Smashing; NN/g).
5. **Grids and margins.** Start from a grid; align, then break it once, on
   purpose (Swiss-style history; brutalism guides on breaking the grid).
6. **White space is structure.** Empty space creates tension and calm; a
   smaller photo framed by space can hit harder (Japanese "ma" write-ups;
   editorial layout guides).
7. **Colour discipline.** A limited palette; one signature colour owned
   everywhere (foodpanda's pink, Glossier's pink, The Economist's red).
8. **Two typefaces at most.** Too many faces looks cluttered (Creative Bloq,
   editorial design rules).
9. **The offer and the action unmissable** — for selling posts, benefit
   headline → proof → one clear call to action (direct-response guides).
10. **Consistency over novelty.** A repeated device (stripes, crop of the
    mark, the same character) builds recognition over many posts (McDonald's
    Canada, Amul, Adidas).
